INCIDENT REPORT // 08 DEC 1994
▸ ANOMALY — SUBJECT MISSING // CASE SUPPRESSED

Grinberg was last seen on 8 December 1994 — the same month his landmark paper was published. He told his daughter he was traveling to Kathmandu for meditation sessions. No airline has a record of him boarding any flight.

His second wife, Teresa Mendoza, disappeared the same day. When investigators entered the residence, all research materials were gone — computers, files, writings: erased.

Comandante Clemente Padilla, the assigned investigator, was removed from the case under pressure from Mexico's Attorney General's Office. A second investigator contradicted Padilla's findings. President Ernesto Zedillo personally requested information on the file. The investigation was called off. Padilla chose retirement rather than compliance.

Grinberg's brother brought the full archive to the police chief hoping to restart the investigation. Three days later, the file had vanished from the police chief's office.

CIA Stargate program documents — declassified August 2023 — reference Grinberg's studies explicitly. The Stargate program was terminated in 1995. One year after Grinberg disappeared.

PROTOCOL 01

AUTO-ALLUSIVE (SELF-REFERENTIAL) MEDITATION

DOCUMENTED · OPERATOR PIPELINE

Grinberg’s signature meditation manual. The technique he used to put subjects into the brain-state that produced the transferred-potential effect, and that he taught publicly in Mexico for two decades. The core move: attention recursively turns onto its own activity, collapsing the subject/object split. This is the “operator pipeline” — the prerequisite condition for everything else in his catalog.

▸ SOURCE DOCUMENTS

La Teoría Sintérgica (Grinberg) · Original Spanish theory volume with meditation chapters Psi Encyclopedia — Grinberg entry · English overview of auto-allusive technique and EEG signature ResearchGate publication index · EEG-coherence studies of meditators using this technique

▸ THE TECHNIQUE (7-stage protocol, ~45 minutes)

  1. Posture & settling (5 min). Seated upright, spine self-supporting, eyes closed or soft-focus. Two minutes of slow diaphragmatic breathing to settle the autonomic baseline.
  2. Body scan (5 min). Move attention through the body head-to-feet, noting sensation without changing it. Goal: get the attentional spotlight working smoothly before turning it on itself.
  3. Object-attention (5 min). Pick one stable percept — breath at the nostrils, or a felt-sense in the chest. Hold attention there. When it wanders, return.
  4. First inversion (10 min). Instead of attending to the breath, attend to the attending itself. Notice the act of noticing. The breath becomes background; the “noticer” becomes the foreground. This will feel unstable and recursive — that is correct.
  5. Second inversion (10 min). Now notice the one who is noticing the noticing. The recursion is the point. Do not try to “arrive” anywhere; rest in the loop itself. Subjects commonly report a felt-sense of the boundary between self and field thinning.
  6. Open lattice (8 min). Release the recursion. Let attention be unfocused, panoramic, equally present to internal and external. Grinberg framed this as “resting in the syntergic lattice.” Phenomenologically: spacious, alert, contentless.
  7. Re-entry (2 min). Slow breath, finger and toe movement, eyes open softly. Sit for 60 seconds before standing.

▸ TRAINING PROGRESSION

  • Weeks 1–4: Stages 1–3 only. Build the attentional baseline. 20-minute sessions, daily.
  • Weeks 5–8: Add stages 4–5. Expect destabilization — this is the recursion working. If you experience persistent dissociation outside the session, back off.
  • Weeks 9+: Full protocol. Move to twice-daily if pursuing the operator pipeline seriously.
  • EEG signature (for those instrumenting): Grinberg reported increased inter-hemispheric coherence at 7–13 Hz (alpha band) during stages 5–6, with frontal-midline theta bursts during inversion transitions. Replicable on consumer EEG.
CAUTION: The recursive-attention stages can trigger depersonalization episodes in vulnerable practitioners. If you have a history of dissociative disorder, psychosis, or unstable trauma, work with a trauma-informed teacher rather than self-directing. Stop the protocol if symptoms persist outside the session.

Multi-operator coherence training. Where Protocol 01 builds the single-operator brain-state, Protocol 02 chains operators into a coupled group. The hypothesis: a sufficiently coherent group locks onto a shared syntergic-field configuration that no single operator could sustain alone.

▸ MINIMUM VIABLE GROUP PROTOCOL

  1. Prerequisite: all participants must be fluent in Protocol 01 (auto-allusive meditation) to at least stage 5. Group coherence collapses if half the group is still doing object-attention.
  2. Group size: 5–12 ideal. Grinberg found returns diminish above ~15 unless tightly facilitated.
  3. Spatial arrangement: seated in a circle, equal spacing, facing inward. Eyes closed. Silent.
  4. Co-induction (15 min): a single facilitator paces breath aloud (4-in, 6-out) until breathing synchronizes audibly across the group. Then silence.
  5. Synchronous auto-allusive run (30 min): all participants execute Protocol 01 stages 3–6 simultaneously. Facilitator gives quiet voice-cues at stage transitions.
  6. Open-field sit (10 min): stage 6 of Protocol 01, held collectively. This is the “resonance window.”
  7. Re-entry & debrief (10 min): open eyes, share felt-quality. Do not interpret — just report.

▸ IF YOU WANT TO INSTRUMENT IT

  • Hyperscanning: 2+ consumer EEG rigs with synced clocks (OpenBCI works). Compute inter-subject phase-locking value (PLV) in alpha (8–12 Hz).

  • Random-effects field detector (REG / quantum RNG): place a hardware RNG in the room, look for deviations from chance during the resonance window. This is the Princeton GCP methodology.
HONEST READ: Group-coherence effects are real at the EEG level (inter-brain synchrony in meditation groups is replicable). Whether that coherence does anything outside the brains in the room is the unresolved claim. Don’t over-interpret subjective field-sensations — collect data.
PROTOCOL 03

BRAIN-TO-BRAIN INTERFACE / TRANSFERRED POTENTIAL

PUBLISHED · 1994

Two subjects in separate electromagnetically-shielded (Faraday) rooms. One receives intermittent visual stimulation. The other’s EEG shows a correlated potential at the same timing — no known signaling channel between them. This is the headline empirical result, published in Physics Essays 1994, and the work most directly aligned with the Stargate-program mandate.

▸ SOURCE DOCUMENTS

PSI ENCYCLOPEDIA — Grinberg entry · Full bibliography, experiment methodology, replication record Grinberg-Zylberbaum et al. (1994) — Physics Essays · “The Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen Paradox in the Brain”

▸ EQUIPMENT (minimum)

  • Two electromagnetically-shielded rooms (Faraday cages). Practically: at minimum two rooms separated by 14+ meters, no shared electrical circuit, and a controlled-timing trigger.
  • Two EEG rigs with synchronized clocks (sub-millisecond timing). Minimum 19-channel 10–20 montage; modern dry-electrode consumer EEGs (Muse, OpenBCI Cyton) work for pilot data but not for publication-grade results.
  • A flash stimulus device (LED panel or strobe) on a controlled timing channel for one subject (the “sender”).
  • Software to extract evoked potentials and run cross-correlation. EEGLAB (MATLAB) or MNE-Python.

▸ PROTOCOL

  1. Pair selection. Recruit pairs with a pre-existing emotional bond (long-term partners, twins, close meditators-in-training). Grinberg consistently found stronger effects in bonded pairs; randomly-paired strangers showed weak-to-null transfer.
  2. Co-meditation induction. Both subjects sit together in the same room for 20 minutes prior to recording. Silent eyes-closed sitting, attention on the other person’s presence. This is the “coupling” phase — the dependent variable Grinberg attributed the effect to.
  3. Separation. Move each subject to their own shielded room. Fit EEG. Maintain instruction to “keep felt-contact” with the other person.
  4. Stimulus block. Sender receives ~100 light flashes at randomized inter-stimulus intervals (5–15 seconds, jittered). Receiver receives no stimulus, eyes closed, instructed to remain in coupling-state.
  5. Analysis. Time-lock the receiver’s EEG to the sender’s flash timestamps. Average across trials. Compare against time-shuffled control (same EEG, randomly re-aligned timestamps). The published result: a small but statistically reliable evoked potential in the receiver at the same latency as the sender’s visual evoked potential.
  6. Controls. Run the same protocol with non-bonded pairs (predicts no effect) and with the sender absent (predicts no effect). Both negative controls are essential.

▸ REPLICATION STATUS

  • Partial replications: Wackermann et al. (2003) Neuroscience Letters — small effect, similar direction. Standish et al. (2004) — positive in bonded pairs.
  • Failed replications also exist. Effect size is small (Cohen’s d ~0.2–0.4 in best runs), so adequately powered studies require n > 30 pairs.
  • This is the most defensible piece of Grinberg’s catalog. If you replicate one thing, replicate this.
NOTE: Without proper electromagnetic shielding and clock synchronization, you cannot rule out trivial explanations (60Hz hum coupling, accidental cueing). A real run requires either institutional EEG facilities or a serious DIY shielded-room build.
PROTOCOL 04

SHAMANIC TRANSMISSION / PACHITA-LINE WORK

ETHNOGRAPHIC · NOT SELF-TRAINABLE

Grinberg’s decade-long apprenticeship with Pachita (Bárbara Guerrero), the Mexican curandera who performed psychic surgeries in front of his EEG instrumentation. This is not a self-trainable protocol — it requires direct lineage transmission from a working practitioner — but Grinberg documented the operator preparation in detail and the observational framework is reproducible.

▸ SOURCE DOCUMENTS

Grinberg — Pachita (full PDF) · Primary ethnographic record of his apprenticeship Psi Encyclopedia — curandera fieldwork section · English overview of the Pachita work

▸ HOW PACHITA WAS TRAINED (lineage)

  • Abandoned in early childhood and adopted by an African man named Charles, who raised her for 14 years and “taught her to read the stars and to heal.” No formal curriculum — pure lineage transmission.
  • Lived through extreme experiential range — fought alongside Pancho Villa’s forces, worked as a cabaret singer and lottery-ticket vendor, sang on buses. Grinberg’s read: this radical compression of life experience is what “connected her with what transcended all of it” — the training was biographical, not didactic.
  • Working modality: she did not perform as herself. Her personality went absent during operations; the entity she called “Hermanito Cuauhtémoc” (spirit of the last Aztec emperor) used her body. She had no memory of the sessions afterward — when Grinberg read his manuscript back to her, she was the most astonished reader.

▸ HOW SHE TAUGHT (apprenticeship method)

  1. Admission by readiness, not application. The doorman (Cuauhtémoc’s instruction) decided who entered. Grinberg waited outside meditating until told: “The Brother wants you to pass.” No preparatory course — you proved ready by showing up.
  2. Throw-in immersion from session one. First operation: assistant duties immediately. No lecture, no demonstration. The Brother addressed him directly (“Mi niño, acércate”), placed him at the operating bedside, and told him what to do mid-procedure.
  3. Embodied instruction (“satura”). Core technique — closing the wound — was transmitted by single word: “Rápido, satura la herida.” Apply alcohol-soaked cotton, lay hands, intend closure. Grinberg writes that he simply “knew” what to do. The teaching was the doing.
  4. Tiered assistant hierarchy. Pachita’s veteran assistant Armando (six+ years, retained ordinary consciousness in exchange for less protection — his own deal with the Brother) and Leo (six years, twice-weekly) modeled the protocol. Her son Memo handled the herbal pharmacopeia and taught its preparation. Apprentices learned laterally from each other as much as from her.
  5. Per-patient adaptation. Treatments were not standardized. Each patient received a uniquely composed herbal regimen, surgical sequence, and consultation. Apprentices absorbed pattern-recognition by watching dozens of variants, not by memorizing a recipe.
  6. Closing teaching ritual. After each night of operations (often ending at midnight), assistants gathered around her, received the Brother’s blessing, asked questions, and got direct advice on their own work and lives. Doctrine was delivered after the work, not before.
  7. Daños framework (taught in parallel by Don Lucio in Tepoztlán, Morelos): the curandero distinction between “good illness” (natural, treatable conventionally) and “daño” (somatic manifestation of another person’s envy or malign intent). Diagnosis and extraction were taught alongside the surgical work.

▸ HOW GRINBERG LEARNED (his discipline as apprentice)

  1. Same-day field notes. He wrote up each session the same night or the following morning — the entire book is built from these contemporaneous notes. He explicitly refused to retouch them years later to preserve “the freshness of the text.”
  2. Participant ethnography, not observation. From the first session he was at the bedside with hands on patients, not behind a clipboard. He performed the “saturation” on incisions himself. He was operated on by Pachita more than once. The data was generated through him.
  3. Travel with the lineage. He followed her to Parral (Chihuahua) where he witnessed the drought-breaking weather work, and ran parallel fieldwork in Tepoztlán (Don Lucio), the Mazatec velada tradition (Psilocybe / Salvia divinorum), Huichol peyote pilgrimages to Wirikuta, and other entheogenic lineages.
  4. Suspension of professional categories. As a psychofisiólogo with a decade of cortical research behind him, he had to actively suspend the “this is impossible” reflex. His method: “decidí no juzgar y simplemente aceptar lo que veía y oía” — record first, theorize later.
  5. Holding contradictions. He stayed explicit that sleight-of-hand theater was interleaved with what he interpreted as genuine effects, and that disentangling them was the unsolved problem of his work. He neither endorsed nor dismissed the Cuauhtémoc-as-discrete-entity claim — he kept it as data.
  6. Translation back to the lab. What he could instrument, he instrumented. Operator EEG signatures, patient coupling effects, and trance-state phenomenology fed directly into his INPEC research program and the syntergic-theory volumes.
  7. Operator preparation he adopted personally. Daily meditation, fasting before sessions, no recreational use of plant medicines (he treated them as instruments), participation in ceremonial structure rather than spectatorship, and the curandera ethic Pachita embedded: ability without service collapses the practitioner.

▸ OBSERVATIONAL FRAMEWORK (what Grinberg actually measured)

  1. Pre-state baseline. EEG on operator (curandera) and patient before any contact.
  2. Operator entrainment. Curandera enters working trance — in Pachita’s case, identified as channeling “Hermanito Cuauhtémoc.” EEG signature: large-amplitude slow waves, frontal theta, reduced reactivity to external stimuli.
  3. Patient coupling. Direct physical contact. Grinberg reported patient EEG drifting toward operator EEG profile within 30–90 seconds — the same coupling signature seen in Protocol 03 but stronger.
  4. Intervention phase. The actual procedure (which in Pachita’s case included sleight-of-hand “surgical” theater alongside reported clinical effects). Grinberg was explicit that the theatrical and the genuine were interleaved and that disentangling them was the unsolved problem.
  5. Post-state. Both EEGs return to baseline. Patient outcomes recorded over weeks/months.
HONEST READ: You cannot “train” yourself into shamanic operator status from a book. What you can do is replicate Grinberg’s observational protocol if you have access to a working traditional healer who consents to instrumentation. The reproducible piece is the coupling-signature measurement (operator→patient EEG drift), not the “powers.” Treat the rest as ethnography, not as engineering.
PROTOCOL 05

EXTRAOCULAR VISION / DERMO-OPTIC PERCEPTION

PUBLISHED · 1983

Identifying colors, shapes, or printed text while fully blindfolded, using skin contact (typically fingertips, forehead, or back of neck). Grinberg published the original protocol in 1983 working with children at the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM). The training works best on subjects under ~14 and degrades with age — he hypothesized the lattice-coupling is more plastic before pubertal cortical pruning.

▸ SOURCE DOCUMENTS

▸ EQUIPMENT

  • Opaque blindfold (sleep mask + black electrical tape over any light leak; verify zero light by waving a flashlight across the eyes).
  • Set of solid-color cards (red / blue / green / yellow / black / white) printed on identical paper stock so weight, texture, and edge-feel are uniform — this controls for tactile leakage.
  • Quiet, evenly-lit room. Lab notebook + timer.
  • A second person (the “runner”) who shuffles and presents cards. The runner must be silent and avoid breath cues.

These are written in columns, not prose — the structure prevents narrative construction. The goal is to accumulate raw perceptual data without the mind assembling it into a recognizable scene. If the viewer starts recognizing a "building" or "ocean," that is Analytical Overlay (AOL) — it is noted separately and bracketed. The signal, not the interpretation, is what Stage 2 captures.

▸ CORE DESIGN PRINCIPLES — WHAT CRV IS BUILT TO PREVENT
ANALYTICAL OVERLAY (AOL)
The single greatest threat to signal integrity. The conscious mind pattern-matches incoming data to known categories too quickly — once it identifies "it's a building," imagination fills in details the signal did not provide. Every CRV stage is designed to delay and quarantine AOL rather than eliminate it. AOL is labeled, noted, set aside — not suppressed.
FRONT-LOADING
Any prior knowledge of the target contaminates the session. CRV uses a fully blind protocol: the viewer knows nothing about the target beyond a random reference number. The monitor (session coordinator) may also be blind to prevent unconscious cueing. This was the core methodological difference between CRV and earlier psychic research that failed replication.
STAGE COMPRESSION
Rushing to the "interesting" stages without properly working Stage 1–2 produces sessions full of imagination and confirmation bias. The staged progression is non-negotiable — each stage builds the signal strength before the next level of data is accessed. This is why untrained viewers produce chance-level results on blind targets.
